# GIPR-Ab/GLP-1 Conjugates for Obesity: A CNS-Dependent Strategy

**Paper:** Liu et al., _Nature Metabolism_, 2025
DOI: 10.1038/s42255-025-01295-w

## Summary

This study by Liu et al. introduces a novel dual-action therapeutic: a peptide-antibody conjugate (GIPR-Ab/GLP-1) that simultaneously antagonizes GIPR and activates GLP-1R to induce sustained weight loss in obese mice. Crucially, the authors show that the full efficacy of this molecule depends on the presence of both CNS GIPR and GLP-1R, indicating that central (brain) mechanisms are critical for its anti-obesity effects. 

## Key Findings
- **Central GIPR antagonism alone** leads to significant weight and food intake reduction in obese mice.
- The **peptide-antibody conjugate accesses the brain via circumventricular organs (CVOs)**; regions not protected by the blood-brain barrier, and activates downstream appetite-regulating regions such as the nucleus of the solitary tract and central amygdala.
- **Genetic deletion of CNS GIPR (GiprSyn-/-)** or CNS GLP-1R (Glp1rWnt1-/-) reduces, but does not abolish, the weight loss induced by GIPR-Ab/GLP-1, suggesting additive, not redundant, roles.
- CNS GIPR knockout mice are resistant to diet-induced obesity, exhibit altered WAT/liver gene expression (lower inflammatory and lipid metabolic markers), and show enhanced response to GLP-1R agonists like dulaglutide.
- **Transcriptomic analyses** in WAT and liver reveal that both CNS GIPR KO and mGIPR-Ab/GLP-1 therapy downregulate cholesterol biosynthesis, extracellular matrix remodeling, and inflammation pathways.
- Despite their large size, peptide-antibody conjugates exert central pharmacodynamic effects, establishing CVOs as viable entry points for CNS drug delivery.
